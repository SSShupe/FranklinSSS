Sunrise was an hour away when Peter Ladouceur awoke in his small home outside of Edmonton. Outside a cold wind blew from the Northwest. Peter reach to his bedside table and turned on the dim lamp, pushed back the thick comforter, and got out of bed. He slowly went down on his knees and began his daily morning prayer.

Peter was a devout evangelical Christian, but he began his prayers with a meditation, a focus on his breathing, in and out, fifteen or so minutes of centering, making himself ready to speak to and hear from God in prayer.

He was following his breath when he felt a light pressure, a tingling, at the top of his spinal cord. It felt to Peter like a sort of wispy, gentle touch.
